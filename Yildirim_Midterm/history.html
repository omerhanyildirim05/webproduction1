HISTORY OF SPACE EXPLORATION

Humanity has always looked toward the stars with curiosity and wonder. What began as observations of the night sky eventually developed into one of mankind's greatest scientific achievements: space exploration.

Through technological advancements, scientific discoveries, and international cooperation, humanity has gradually expanded its understanding of the universe and our place within it.

1957 — THE SPACE RACE

On October 4, 1957, the Soviet Union launched Sputnik 1, the world's first artificial satellite. This achievement marked the beginning of the Space Age and intensified competition between the United States and the Soviet Union.

The competition encouraged rapid advances in rocket technology, satellite communication, and scientific research. In 1958, the United States established NASA to lead its civilian space exploration efforts.

1969 — THE MOON

On July 20, 1969, NASA's Apollo 11 mission successfully landed humans on the Moon for the first time. Astronauts Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin explored the lunar surface while Michael Collins remained in orbit.

This historic achievement represented a major milestone in human exploration and demonstrated how far space technology had advanced in just a few decades.

1998 — INTERNATIONAL SPACE STATION

Construction of the International Space Station began in 1998 through cooperation between multiple nations. The station became a permanent research laboratory orbiting Earth.

Astronauts from different countries conduct scientific experiments aboard the station, studying subjects such as biology, physics, and the effects of living in microgravity.

LEARN MORE

2021 — DISCOVERING DISTANT WORLDS

On December 25, 2021, the James Webb Space Telescope launched into space. Developed through an international partnership between NASA, ESA, and the Canadian Space Agency, Webb was designed to observe the universe using infrared light.

Its observations help scientists study distant galaxies, the formation of stars, and planetary systems beyond our own. It represents another major step toward understanding the universe.
